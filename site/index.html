A house style for subsurface stories

One palette logic, one type system, one way of stating sources and confidence, and one story that reads as an article or plays as a deck.

Every colour on this page is generated from seven anchor hues (eight with amethyst), a shared 15-step lightness ladder and a chroma curve. Contrast is checked automatically. Switch theme, type pairing and units above. Everything you see responds.

02 · Colour

Every hue, the same fifteen steps

Each scale is oklch(Lstep, gaussian(L) × Canchor, Hanchor). Lightness is shared, so step 13 is always body text and step 9 always an emphasis fill, whatever the hue. Adding a colour means adding one anchor. Hover a swatch for its value.

04 · Data colour

Context in grey, the story in colour

Two palettes for two jobs. The story palette is quiet and on-brand: one to three series beside grey context, always labelled directly. The categorical palette is for real identity (operators, for instance). It passes the lightness, chroma, colour-vision and contrast checks in both themes, in a fixed order.

06 · Read and Present

One story, two ways to tell it

Scroll to read: each card moves the chart to its next state. Or press Present: the same states become slides (arrow keys, space, swipe, Esc to leave), with the same d3 transitions.

Step 1

Discoveries came in waves

Counting discoveries by decade shows a rise to a peak, then a long tail. The chart starts neutral: no colour until there is something to see.

Step 2

The 1990s were the busiest decade

One highlight, one annotation. Everything else stays as context.

Step 3

But the finds got smaller

The same decades, now sized by average discovery volume. The bars keep their identity as the measure changes, and the axis rescales first so you can follow it.

Step 4

Busiest is not biggest

Ranked by number of finds, the 1990s lead and the 1960s fall to last. Each bar slides to its new place rather than being redrawn. This is object constancy: the reader never loses track of a decade.
