Three short lessons on how a mature basin behaves: how its fields produce, what is still found in it, and when
its wells are drilled. A demonstration of SubScout's deck mode on invented data: each view is one slide the size
of the screen, and nothing on a slide scrolls.

## How a field produces

A field is a finite volume. Its production follows from that: a ramp while wells come on stream, a plateau set
by the capacity of the installation, and a decline once the wells can no longer fill it.

::: step
### Ramp, plateau and decline

```concept
name: Exponential decline
formula: 'q_t = q_{t-1}\,(1 - d)'
where: '$q_t$ production in year $t$, $d$ the share lost each year'
```

Field A is typical of the basin. Production climbs for three years, holds for a short plateau and then falls
by a constant share each year. The typical plateau here lasts {{ typical_plateau | round:0 }} years.

:::

::: step
### A long plateau is the exception

Field C held its plateau for {{ longest_plateau }} years. A long plateau means the installation, not the
reservoir, limited production for most of the field's life.

```chart
use: production
focus: [Field C]
title: Field C held its plateau for {{ longest_plateau }} years
```
:::

::: step
### A tie-back fills spare capacity

Once Field C came off plateau its installation had room. Field F was produced through it, and the pair
outlasted their neighbours: Field C still gives {{ share_2025_c | pct }} of the basin's output in the last
year of the record.

```chart
use: production
focus: [Field C, Field F]
note: { name: Field F, x: 2016, text: "Tie-back to Field C" }
title: Fields C and F outlasted their neighbours
```
:::

## What is still found

::: step

::: step
### More finds, less volume

The busiest decade brought {{ peak_decade_finds }} discoveries, but small ones. A find of the latest decade
holds {{ avg_2020s | about }} Sm³ o.e. on average, which pays only as a tie-back to an existing installation.

:::

## When the wells are drilled

Exploration is paid for out of cash flow, so the number of wells follows the price of oil with a short delay.

::: step
### Drilling follows the price

Quarterly wells fell {{ wells_drop | pct }} after the price fall of 2015, and dipped again in 2020. Small
prospects near infrastructure are the first to be postponed and the first to return.
